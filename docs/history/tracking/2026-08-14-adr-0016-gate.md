# Architecture Gate — ADR-0016 (Adoption is the standing request for agent invocation)

**Date:** 2026-08-14 · **Lens:** Archie (architecture) · **Parallel:** Wei (devil's advocate) · **Authorised by:** the human, directly, 2026-08-14

**Headline:** the decision is sound. Every defect I found is in how the decision is *stated*, not in what it decides. Four amendments, none of them blocking.

---

## 1. Is the narrow scope correct?

**Verdict: needs change.**

The refusal is right. A project file that asserts supremacy over the runtime it executes in is a category error, and it would be wrong the first time a restriction deserved to win — cost caps, consent gates, safety interlocks. The ADR rejects the general-precedence alternative for exactly this reason and the rejection holds.

But the boundary is drawn around the **instance** rather than around the **predicate**, and that is what will let the collision recur. § *Context* diagnoses the root defect precisely — *"Summon has never said where a runtime-injected session instruction sits relative to its own canon, so every such collision is resolved from scratch by whoever hits it"* — and then § *Decision* fixes one instance of it. The next restriction shaped `do not write outside cwd unless the user requested it`, or `do not run background tasks unless requested`, has an identical shape and an identical answer, and this ADR gives a coordinator no purchase on it whatsoever. Round three arrives on schedule.

There is a third position available between "one instance" and "`CLAUDE.md` wins," and it is not the alternative the ADR rejected. Generalize the **predicate**, not the **authority**:

- Where an injected restriction is **conditioned on a user request**, adoption satisfies that condition for the behaviours the adopted canon specifies.
- Where a restriction is **unconditional**, or conditioned on anything other than a user request, it wins, and the collision goes to the human.

That is strictly narrower than the rejected alternative — it cannot reach a safety interlock, because a safety interlock is not phrased as *unless the user asked* — and it resolves the whole family instead of one member.

It also fixes a real imprecision in the shipped text. § *Decision* currently says *"state the conflict in one line and proceed,"* which concedes a conflict exists. **On the ADR's own logic, there isn't one.** If adoption is the request, then the restriction's own condition has been **met**. Nothing is being overridden. That distinction is the entire reason the narrow scope is principled rather than arbitrary, and the current wording gives it away in the one sentence a coordinator will actually act on. A coordinator that believes it is overriding the runtime is one bad inference from believing it may override the runtime generally.

**Amendment 1.** Restate the operative rule as the two-part predicate above, and change *"state the conflict"* to language that says the condition is satisfied — the conflict is apparent, not real. Add one line to § *Alternatives Considered* distinguishing this from the rejected general-precedence claim, so a later reader does not mistake the amendment for the thing the ADR turned down.

## 2. Is `ungraded` correctly placed on the proof ladder?

**Verdict: needs change.**

It is a type error, and the ADR trips over it in a single paragraph. § *Decision* says *"The Done Gate gains a third **outcome**"* and then, two sentences later, *"This adds a fourth term to the **proof ladder**."* Those are two different structures. The document does not know which one it is extending.

The three grades answer *how was this claim established?* Their domain is established claims. `ungraded` answers *why is there no claim?* — a question about the item, not about any claim, because there is no claim to grade. The gloss offered in the shipped text, *"Not a grade of the claim; a grade of the evidence's absence,"* is reaching for this and lands wrong: it is not a grade of anything. That phrasing preserves the type error while apologising for it, which is worse than either fixing it or ignoring it.

The correct shape is two levels, and it is already implicit in how the gate is used:

- **Outcome** — `satisfied` or `ungraded` (reason named).
- **Grade** — for satisfied items only: `deterministic` / `inferential` / `human-judgement`.

This is not pedantry, because the vocabulary is shared and the fork has a named downstream victim. ADR-0015 binds the packet's `epistemic` enum to this same ladder deliberately — *"the same three grades as the Done Gate's proof ladder, because it is the same ladder. One vocabulary, two subjects"* — and states *"These three are the whole set... nothing else is valid under `v: 1`."* Grow the gate ladder to four entries and one of two things happens: either `schemas/packet.schema.json` needs a `v: 2` carrying an `epistemic: "ungraded"` that is **meaningless for a claim**, or the two documents silently diverge and the "same ladder" assertion in ADR-0015 becomes false with nothing to catch it.

The decisive argument is that **PACKET already solved this exact problem, correctly.** A thing the agent could not determine does not become a fourth `epistemic` value; it goes in `unknowns[]`, a separate channel from `claims[]`, mandatory and possibly empty. That is the same structural move — absence gets its own slot rather than contaminating the grade enum — and the Done Gate should mirror the shape that already works instead of inventing a different answer to the same question.

**Amendment 2.** Recast `ungraded` as an item **outcome**, not a ladder entry. State the two-level structure in `done-gate.md`. Add an explicit line that the three-value ladder remains closed and `schemas/packet.schema.json`'s `epistemic` enum is unchanged at `v: 1`. Drop the "grade of the evidence's absence" gloss.

## 3. Does `ungraded` create a bypass?

**Verdict: needs change.**

Yes, and the Reversal guard is insufficient in one specific, fixable way: **it is a retrospective trigger with no sensor behind it.**

§ *Reversal* fires when ungraded closes become *"routine."* Counted by whom, from what? § *Consequences* claims the property outright — *"Naming `ungraded` as a ladder entry makes an absent check countable. A project that accumulates them can see it"* — and ships no counter. There is no named location where gate records live in machine-readable form, no threshold, no command. This repository has been bitten by precisely this before: three Done Gate items graded `deterministic` with no command behind them. An ADR that asserts a measurable reversal trigger and provides no measurement is the same defect wearing a Reversal section.

Note also that the ADR's own instrument choice argues against leaving this to prose. It rejected per-user memory **empirically**, on a demonstrated failure rate. A guard that depends on someone noticing a pattern across sessions is the same class of instrument.

The structural limit should go in at introduction, and the cheapest one doubles as the missing sensor:

- **A closed list of admissible reasons.** § *Decision* half-offers one — tool absent, spawn denied, restriction the standing request does not reach. Close it. Everything else goes to the human. Name **context exhaustion as inadmissible** explicitly, because it is the reason that will be reached for, and it is a scoping failure whose fix is a smaller wave. ADR-0015 refuses an escape hatch on narrative length for exactly this reason: the pressure is the signal, and suppressing it removes the only feedback that produces the real fix.
- **The reason quotes the blocking mechanism verbatim** — the injected text, the tool name, the error — not a paraphrase. Makes the record auditable later and makes fabrication cost something.
- **`ungraded` is unavailable when the human is reachable.** If a person can be asked, the exit is asking. `ungraded` is for genuinely blocked, not for inconvenient.
- **An ungraded close records a follow-up work item.** This is the important one. *"Permanent legible hole"* is right about the **record** and wrong if it becomes true of the **gap** — code that closed unreviewed stays unreviewed unless something re-queues it. And the follow-up item **is the counter the Reversal section needs**: open ungraded-follow-ups on the board, no new script, existing machinery.

**Amendment 3.** Add the four limits above to § *Decision*, and rewrite the Reversal trigger to name the count it fires on (open ungraded follow-up items) rather than the word "routine."

## 4. Is the self-application in Status sound?

**Verdict: sound.** No amendment beyond bookkeeping.

Not circular. Circularity would require the ADR to **discharge** its own gate using the `ungraded` outcome it is proposing — the new escape hatch's first use being the ADR that creates it, which would have been the worst available precedent and a genuine blocker. It does not do that. It **blocks**, which is the honest move and the one its own § *Decision* asks of everyone else. The consistency is to the document's credit and I want that on the record.

One correction. § *Status* says the drafting session *"could not spawn them without the request whose absence is the subject matter,"* which overstates the bind. The human can request the gate directly — which is exactly what happened on 2026-08-14. The block was never permanent; it was one sentence away. The impasse the ADR describes is real for **routine work items**, where asking every time *is* the failure being fixed. It is not real for a **one-off ratification**, where a single explicit request is cheap and entirely appropriate.

That distinction is worth preserving in the record, because it is the cleanest statement of why this ADR is not bootstrapping itself into existence: it is ratified by an external human act, not by self-application of its own rule.

**Amendment 4 (bookkeeping, at ratification).** Update § *Status* to Accepted; record the gate discharged 2026-08-14 by direct human authorisation, naming Archie and Wei; soften the "could not spawn them" claim to distinguish the routine case from the one-off ratification.

## 5. Placement — canon or meta?

**Verdict: placement sound; needs change (missing classification argument).**

Canon, unambiguously. The test is whether the decision governs projects scaffolded from Summon or only the Summon repository itself. ADR-0016's decision text lands in `CLAUDE.md` and `docs/process/done-gate.md`, both of which ship. Every downstream project inherits the collision — the restriction is injected at session launch and appears in no settings file, so it is universal, not local. And the ADR's own central argument for choosing this instrument over per-user memory is precisely that it ships: *"Every project scaffolded from Summon inherits it, which is the property per-user memory lacked and the reason this failure recurred."* That sentence is the classification argument. It is sitting in § *Context* doing other work.

The defect is corpus-level, not local. ADR-0015 classifies itself explicitly in a § *Classification* section; ADR-0016 asserts canon by implication. A corpus where classification is sometimes argued and sometimes inferred degrades the ADR-0007 boundary into a convention no reader can check and no script could ever check — a misplacement becomes invisible, because there is nothing to be wrong. Every ADR after 0007 should classify itself, and the fix here is one paragraph reusing an argument the document has already made.

**Amendment 5, folded into Amendment 4's pass.** Add a § *Classification* section mirroring ADR-0015's, promoting the ships-with-scaffold argument out of § *Context*.

## Overall recommendation

**Ratify with amendments.** Five, listed above; none blocks the decision, and all five change text rather than substance:

1. **Generalize the predicate, not the authority** — request-conditioned restrictions are *satisfied* by adoption; unconditional ones win and go to the human. Fix *"state the conflict"* accordingly.
2. **`ungraded` is an outcome, not a fourth ladder term** — two-level structure; the three-grade ladder stays closed; `packet.schema.json`'s `epistemic` enum unchanged at `v: 1`; drop the "grade of the evidence's absence" gloss.
3. **Structural limits on `ungraded` at introduction** — closed reason list (context exhaustion inadmissible), verbatim mechanism quoted, unavailable when a human is reachable, follow-up item required; retarget the Reversal trigger at that follow-up count.
4. **Status bookkeeping at ratification** — Accepted; gate discharged 2026-08-14 by direct human authorisation; distinguish routine blocking from one-off ratification.
5. **Add § *Classification*** — canon, arguing it explicitly as ADR-0015 does.

The core decision holds under pressure. Adoption *is* the standing request; the narrow scope is right in kind even where it is too narrow in statement; and the third gate outcome is a genuine improvement over an honest deadlock and a silent skip. Amendments 2 and 3 are the ones I would not ratify without — one keeps a shared vocabulary from forking, the other keeps a new escape hatch from shipping without a limit or a counter.

## Critical

**W1 — `ungraded` breaks a contract ADR-0015 closed (`0016:41`).** `AGENTS.md` states the packet `epistemic` field uses "the same three grades as the Done Gate's proof ladder … because it is the same ladder", and that "these three are the whole set … nothing else is valid under `v: 1`". Adding a fourth rung is therefore either a breaking schema change requiring `v: 2` and an ADR-0015 amendment, or it falsifies the same-ladder claim. Both are ADR events the draft took neither of. The Neutral consequence "no existing gate item changes its grade" does not cover *adding a rung*. Clean escape, half-written in the draft already: `ungraded` answers *was it checked?*, the grades answer *how do you know?* — different axis, so make it a separate outcome. **Coordinator note:** Wei flagged as unknown whether the schema enumerates the grades as a closed enum. It does — `schemas/packet.schema.json:63-64` — so this was a hard schema break, not a documentation break. **Applied:** Sub-decision 3, outcome above grade, ladder closed at three, schema untouched.

**W2 — the Reversal condition has no counter (`0016:96`, `:78`).** The trigger fires when `ungraded` becomes "routine", and nothing counts them; the draft said a project "*can*" see accumulation. This repo has `harvest:debt` and `harvest:packets` and knows how to build a counter. Worse, the ledger would be written by the same coordinator whose compliance is in question — the argument the ADR itself makes when disclaiming enforcement, not applied to its own reversal trigger. Wei's framing: in a repo whose prior session produced a note titled *sensors that cannot fail*, this would be the sixth instance, shipped in the ADR that introduces the mechanism. **Applied:** every ungraded close opens a follow-up board item; the trigger now reads that count at sprint boundary; the sensor's weakness is stated rather than papered over.

**W3 — the Decision held two incompatible framings and shipped the weaker (`0016:37` vs `:35`, `:39`).** Supplying the restriction's condition is a genuinely narrow operation with an intrinsic stopping point. "Where a session restriction *contradicts* this … proceed" is an override, which is the general-precedence claim the ADR spends a paragraph rejecting. Wei: *"a coordinator that thinks it is overriding the runtime is one bad inference from thinking it may."* Fixing the framing also closes the slippery-slope objection structurally rather than by promise, which is the only way slopes ever get closed. **Applied:** Sub-decisions 1 and 2, and the shipped text.

## Important

**W4 — adoption-as-request manufactures consent downstream (`0016:35` vs issue #82).** "The artifact the user adopted is the evidence" is false where the scaffolder installs Summon's `CLAUDE.md` over a repository whose owner never chose it: artifact present, human never spoke. Narrow scope answers *what* was authorised, never *who*. **Disposition: ruled by the human on 2026-08-14** — adoption does constitute the request, downstream included. Recorded as Sub-decision 4 with the objection kept open against issue #82 rather than closed.

**W5 — the ADR's own taxonomy predicts it loses (`0016:29`, `:75`, `:83`).** Memory is background context and the injected restriction is an active constraint — but `CLAUDE.md` is background context too. The draft argued *distribution* and reused it as though it were *salience*, then promised in Positive what it disclaimed in Negative. **Applied:** the salience argument is now made explicitly (unconditionally present, standing directive to a role, adjacent to the gate it modifies), and the Positive consequence is stated as an expectation rather than a guarantee.

**W6 — load-bearing hearsay stated as fact (`0016:27`).** The recurrence carries the instruments argument, the empirical rejection of memory, and a Negative consequence, and was stated in bold with no provenance marker. Wei's sharpest sub-point: *"read that memory" may itself be an inference from the file existing* — if retrieval merely failed, memory was never tested and "fix retrieval" is a live alternative. **Applied:** provenance paragraph in Context; the unestablished detail is named as unestablished.

**W7 — the test behind the ADR-0015 amendment is unfalsifiable.** Split decision on the secondary target. Wei ruled the *outcome* right — ADR-0015 licensed a document-**reachability** principle, #127's fix asserts **value completeness**, which is strictly wider, so delegation of text is not delegation of scope and the amendment at `98c170d` stands. But "a change to the mechanism is an ADR event" is unfalsifiable: *mechanism* is undefined and any rewording can be narrated as one after the fact, turning ADR-0015 into a changelog. Falsifiable replacement: *does the delegated document now assert something the ADR did not license?* — **citable** (see addendum; the coordinator first transcribed this as *greppable*, which overclaims), same answer for #127, and correctly silent on future wording changes. Better still, state Sub-decision 5's limits inline. **Not applied here** — it amends ADR-0015 on PR #130 and is tracked as a follow-up.

**W8 — agent-notes `state: canonical` on a Proposed ADR (`0016:2`).** Confirmed. `CLAUDE.md` instructs agents to orient from agent-notes, so a later agent reading only front matter would conclude this was ratified canon. **Applied** — now `accepted`, matching Status.

# Addendum, 2026-08-14 — after the gate, on W7

Post-gate and second-hand: a downstream project put Wei's W7 to its own Archie, who struck his own test and refined Wei's replacement. Recorded here rather than in the ADR because it concerns **maintaining ADR-0015**, not ADR-0016. Both corrections below were verified in this tree before being recorded.

**The defect is non-discrimination, not unfalsifiability.** *"The constraint the text must satisfy"* is written down in no artifact, so applying the test means composing the thing you measure against while measuring it. Any change can be redescribed one level up, and every level-up description is a constraint. Wei's charge was right about the conclusion and imprecise about the mechanism.

**"Greppable" was an overclaim, and it is the coordinator's, not Wei's.** In this repo *greppable* implies a check can run it. **Verified: nothing does.** `scripts/check-canon.mjs` asserts that agent files match the canonical text (the `register binding` check, `:251-263`); it never asserts that the canonical text satisfies ADR-0015. **That relation has no check in either direction.** The accurate word is **citable** — both sides have addresses, so a third party can check the verdict and overturn it. That is a real gain and it is not mechanisation. Introducing a rigour fix with an overclaim about its own enforcement is the failure mode wearing the costume of the fix.

**Wei's test has a polarity hole, and it is over the founding case.** *"Asserts something the ADR did not license"* catches a delegated document **gaining** a rule and is silent on one **losing** a rule. **Verified in this tree:** ADR-0015 Sub-decision 5's own Amendment 2 records the originating incident as contraction — *"a later reader given only the paragraph above would 'simplify' the line back to a reference — and before that check existed, doing so passed CI."* The forward-only test is quiet on exactly the failure the sub-decision exists to catch.

Run in reverse, the case resolves without a new rule: an edit that satisfies the new delegated text while violating a sentence in the ADR is **not an amendment at all**, it is a conformance failure, and the remedy is to fix the delegated document rather than amend the ADR.

**Inline limits are the test's input, not a competing option.** The construction's first step is *name the sentence constituting the licence*; a limits list is that sentence, pre-written. Two consequences worth carrying: the list must state that **silence in it is not a licence** — a rule it did not anticipate routes to the construction rather than being permitted, and where the construction fires the list gains an entry in that same amendment, so it is a floor that grows rather than a ceiling that expires. And relocating the judgement to authoring time is a gain rather than a wash, because **limits drafted before a dispute cannot be tuned to win it.**

**Disposition unchanged.** Wei contested the test, not the outcome. `98c170d` stands, on the reachability-vs-completeness reason rather than the struck one — that reason wins because it comes with an *object*: a line naming `agent` as a bare key satisfies the reachability licence completely and still fails #127, and the counterexample is the line that actually shipped.

**Applied to PR #130.** Unchanged and deliberately so: `docs/process/communication-registers.md`, `AGENTS.md`, `schemas/packet.schema.json`, and every agent file. This is a rule about maintaining ADR-0015; all of those sit downstream of the contract's content rather than of its maintenance.

**Open:** whether the ADR-to-delegated-document conformance relation is mechanisable at all. The downstream read is no, without having designed against it. UNRESOLVED here too.
